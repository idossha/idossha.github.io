## Sleep-Related Respiratory Disruption and Spindle Morphology in Children

_SLEEP 2026 · Ido Haber, Tamara P. Taporoski, Camilla Matthews, Emma Strainis,
Ana Maria Vascan, Stephanie G. Jones — Center for Sleep and Consciousness &
Department of Biomedical Engineering, University of Wisconsin–Madison._

Sleep-disordered breathing (SDB) affects roughly 1–5% of children and, even in mild
forms, is linked to neurocognitive impairment. This study asked whether respiratory
disruption during sleep alters the morphology of sleep spindles — thalamocortical
oscillations of NREM sleep — and whether those changes track daytime attention.

Using overnight 256-channel high-density EEG with concurrent polysomnography in 62
children, per-channel regressions related each spindle metric to the hypopnea index
(age- and sex-adjusted), with cluster-based permutation correction. Two effects
survived correction: **shorter anterior fast-spindle duration** and slower anterior
slow-spindle frequency with rising hypopnea index. Anterior fast-spindle duration
was in turn the most consistent predictor of attention (TOVA) — higher d-prime and
fewer omission and commission errors. The functionally relevant abnormality appears
to be a loss of temporal stability in anterior fast spindles, not a failure to
generate spindles — a candidate physiological readout linking respiratory burden to
attentional vulnerability.
